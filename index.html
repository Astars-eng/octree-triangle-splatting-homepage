We keep the triangle-based rendering pipeline and extend it with an explicit octree hierarchy. The goal is to render only the view-dependent subset of triangles that matters for the current camera, while preserving reconstruction quality close to the full Triangle Splatting model.

Abstract

This project modifies the original Triangle Splatting codebase by replacing its unstructured triangle soup with an octree-structured representation. Triangle primitives are still used as the basic radiance-field elements, but they are organized into spatial cells and converted into a coarse-to-fine residual Level-of-Detail hierarchy. During rendering, the system adaptively selects triangle subsets according to camera view, projected cell size, local geometric/color complexity, and visibility.
We further implement a LoD-only checkpoint for inference. Instead of loading the full triangle tensors, inference only loads octree metadata and LoD models. On the drjohnson scene, the LoD-only adaptive renderer keeps SSIM/PSNR/LPIPS nearly identical to the adaptive full-checkpoint version while rendering only 10.76% of triangles on average. Compared with full rendering, checkpoint size decreases from about 2255 MB to 1158 MB, and peak allocated GPU memory drops from about 7847 MB to 6664 MB.

Methodology

The pipeline first trains the original full Triangle Splatting model with densification and pruning, so the scene obtains a complete triangle-based representation. After that, each triangle is assigned to an octree cell according to its center point. For an octree depth \(d\), the scene is divided into \(2^d \times 2^d \times 2^d\) cells, which gives us an explicit spatial structure for grouping, sampling, and view-dependent selection.

Inside each occupied cell, representative triangles are selected with patch-aware statistics instead of using position alone. We compute normal direction, triangle area, feature variance, visibility importance, and a combined detail score. Triangles in the same cell are lightly clustered by normal and feature bins, so different surface directions or color regions are not incorrectly merged.

We build a residual LoD hierarchy over selected octree depths. For example, with depths \(11, 12, 13\), depth 11 is the coarse base layer, depth 12 stores residual triangles not covered by the base, and depth 13 stores finer residual details. Rendering a view becomes:

\( \mathrm{render\_model}(\mathrm{view}) = \mathrm{base\_layer} + \mathrm{selected\_residual\_layers}(\mathrm{view}) \)

Adaptive LoD selection estimates the projected screen size of each cell:

\( \mathrm{projected\_pixels} \approx \frac{\mathrm{cell\_size}}{\mathrm{distance\_to\_camera}} \cdot \mathrm{focal\_scale} \)

Larger projected cells receive finer residual layers, while small or distant regions use coarser layers. A detail boost from normal variance, color/feature variance, and visibility importance preserves high-frequency or historically visible regions even when they are farther from the camera.

To reduce popping between levels, residual layers use a smooth transition weight:

\( w = \mathrm{smoothstep}(t(1-r),\, t(1+r),\, \mathrm{projected\_pixels} \cdot \mathrm{detail\_boost}) \)

We also add frustum culling so cells outside the current camera view do not participate in the LoD composition. During LoD training, the adaptive render is optimized against both ground truth images and the full model render through full-model distillation:

\( L = L_{\mathrm{image}}(\mathrm{LoD}, \mathrm{GT}) + \lambda_{\mathrm{distill}} L_{\mathrm{image}}(\mathrm{LoD}, \mathrm{Full}) + L_{\mathrm{reg}} \)

Finally, we export a LoD-only checkpoint containing only lod_models, octree_lod_depths, sample indices/centers, octree metadata, and the required spherical-harmonic metadata. This removes the full triangle tensors from inference while preserving adaptive residual rendering.

Adaptive Behavior and Limitations

RGB image
Normal map
RGB image
Normal map

Adaptive LoD is view-dependent rather than a fixed-ratio pruning strategy. Across 33 test views, the rendered ratio ranges from 1.72% to 43.83%, with rendered triangle counts from 68,821 to 1,753,330. The current limitation is that LoD selection/precomputation still has noticeable overhead; after amortizing precompute over only 33 views, end-to-end time can be higher than full rendering. Some views also retain local blur or geometric errors inherited from the full Triangle Splatting model.
